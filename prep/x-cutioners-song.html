Lead with this — it is the story of both books

The band walked out and the people left behind had to play the gig. Between page one of the X-Men volume and page 210, Whilce Portacio draws his last Uncanny (#290) and Jim Lee draws his last X-Men (#11). Both are gone to found Image, where Rob Liefeld had already gone from X-Force. Marvel's answer, ordered from the top, is a twelve-part weekly crossover through all four books — written and drawn by whoever was still in the building: Scott Lobdell, Fabian Nicieza, Peter David, and four artists nobody had ever bought seven million copies of. Brandon Peterson, Andy Kubert, Jae Lee, Greg Capullo.

Nicieza says so himself in the 1994 introduction reprinted at the back of both books. He calls these the post-Lee, post-Liefeld, post-Portacio comics, compares the team to musicians picking up instruments somebody else left on stage, and admits he wishes they could go back and do the whole story again. And the X-Men Epic still goes out under a Jim Lee cover — to an issue that has nothing to do with the crossover on the spine.

So the spine for both episodes: did the replacements pull it off? And underneath it, the question the story itself asks and famously will not answer — who is Cable?

01Two books, one story

These volumes overlap by more than half. Worth saying plainly on air, because anyone buying both is buying the crossover twice.

  • Only in X-Men Epic 21: the nine issues before the crossover — Uncanny #289–293 and X-Men #10–13. Storm and Forge, Mojo, the end of the Morlocks, Xavier's father. 205 pages.
  • In both, page for page: the twelve chapters of the Song.
  • Only in X-Force Epic 2: the prologue (Cable: Blood & Metal) and the aftermath (Stryfe's Strike File, New Warriors #31, X-Force #19). About 170 pages.
  • In neither: Uncanny #297, the quiet day-after epilogue. Expect it at the front of X-Men Epic 22: Legacies, already on the plan.

Which file to put on screen. The X-Men file is a standard-definition scan — pages 1,024 pixels wide. The X-Force file is about twice that. For the twelve shared chapters, share the X-Force file; it will be visibly sharper in the comic pane.

A split for two recordings. Part 1, Before the Song: the nine X-Men issues, then Cable: Blood & Metal — 11 comics, about 290 pages. Part 2, The Song: the twelve chapters and the three aftermath books — 15 comics, about 365 pages. If Part 2 looks long, the natural break is after chapter 4, the end of the first month, with X-Force in custody.

03Part one — before the Song

Credits exactly as printed on each splash page

Uncanny X-Men #289
Knots
X-Men Epic p. 5 · June 1992
writer Scott Lobdell · pencils Whilce Portacio · inks Scott Williams · letters Tom Orzechowski · colours Joe Rosas

A day off at the mansion. Storm gives Bishop his orientation. Forge, the man who can build anything, cannot knot Iceman's bow tie. Mystique — staying at the mansion in Forge's care — turns herself into the old feathered Angel to cheer up Archangel and nearly gets cut in half for it. Forge asks Storm to marry him and leave the X-Men. Bobby takes Opal Tanaka to meet his parents, and his father is openly hostile to her — and then Hiro, the cyborg samurai who once kidnapped her, arrives to say he is there to protect them.

Talk about: a whole issue with no villain until the last page — this is Lobdell's signature mode and you will either love it or not. Bobby's father as a bigot starts here and becomes a long-running Iceman thread. The title does triple duty: the bow tie, tying the knot, and everybody's stomach.

Uncanny X-Men #290
Frayed
X-Men Epic p. 28 · July 1992
plot & script Scott Lobdell · pencils Whilce Portacio · inks Scott Williams · letters Lois Buhalis · colours Kevin Tinsley

Iceman fights the Cyburai — cybernetic yakuza enforcers from Opal's own family — in the wreck of the restaurant, and Hiro blows himself up to stop them. Colossus's brother Mikhail is hearing the followers he got killed in the void (Uncanny #285–286, your Epic 20). Storm decides she will say yes. Forge, sure she never will, tells her she is still the orphan under the rubble and walks out with Mystique. The last page is Storm alone, saying what she was going to say.

Talk about: Portacio's last issue. Who is in the wrong, Forge or Storm? He proposes and withdraws it inside two issues. That final page is as good as anything in the book.

X-Men #10 and #11
Where Happy Little Bluebirds Fly… / The X-Men vs. the X-Men! (Again)
X-Men Epic pp. 50 and 74 · July–Aug 1992
#10: billed as a Jim Lee joint, script Scott Lobdell, inks Scott Williams with Bob Wiacek, Karl Altstaetter & Dan Panosian · #11: Jim Lee, Scott Lobdell, inks Bob Wiacek, letters Lois Buhalis, colours Marie Javins

Mojo has the Blue team. For ratings he has recast them in old Earth movies — #10 opens as a Wizard of Oz pastiche with an amnesiac Longshot falling into it — and then sets X-Man against X-Man on live television. Dazzler and Lila Cheney are with the rebels. A clone calling himself Mojo II: The Sequel offers to help. Xavier and Psylocke break the control, Longshot kills Mojo, and Xavier senses that Dazzler is pregnant. Longshot and Dazzler stay behind to rebuild. Jubilee gets the last word: are we being played?

Talk about: this is Jim Lee's goodbye — a story about performers trapped making content for a boss who only cares about ratings, published the same summer he left to own his work. Read as a resignation letter it is hard to unsee. #11 is also the cover on the front of the book.

Maverick back-ups
Last Stand / Over… Again
X-Men Epic pp. 67 and 90 · in X-Men #10–11
writer Scott Lobdell · art Mark Texeira · colours Mike Rockwitz · letters Lois Buhalis

Billed as Maverick's first solo adventure. He is bodyguarding a scientist named Ryking against the armoured Warhawk, and what everybody wants to know about is a missing file on Xavier. Fourteen pages across two issues, and it exists to set up #12–13.

Talk about: Texeira's scratchy, heavy style against Jim Lee's polish in the same comic. Maverick is three issues old at this point (he arrived in X-Men #5).

Uncanny X-Men #291
Underbelly
X-Men Epic p. 98 · Aug 1992
writer Scott Lobdell · guest pencils Tom Raney · guest inks Hilary Barta · letters Lois Buhalis · colours Joe Rosas

Callisto is carried to the mansion half dead. Since Masque died (in X-Force) the Morlocks have gone mad. The Healer spends the last of his power on her — and it strips away the beauty Masque had given her along with the wounds. Archangel blames Storm's absentee leadership of the Morlocks for the night he lost his wings. In the tunnels, a claustrophobic Storm is mobbed and brings down a lightning bolt that opens the street above. The Morlocks are loose in Manhattan.

Talk about: the one-page interlude on p. 109 — a blond man in a suit visits a strapped-down patient at a nursing home in Arlington, Virginia and talks about him as the last hope of the human race. Nothing in this book explains it. By later accounts the patient is Steven Lang, the Sentinel builder from the 1970s, and this is the first seed of the Phalanx, two years early.

Uncanny X-Men #292
…The Morlocks Take Manhattan!
X-Men Epic p. 120 · Sept 1992
writer Scott Lobdell · layouts Rurik Tyler · pencils Tom Raney · inks Josef Rubinstein & Al Milgrom · colours Steve Buccellato

Colossus, Bishop and Jean try to contain the Morlocks in the rain without hurting them. Jean has to shut down the mind of one who is absorbing bystanders into his own body. The NYPD want Bishop for the killings in #287. At the mansion Mikhail shows Iceman what he could be — not a man in an ice shell but ice all the way through — then leaves with Callisto. Xavier, tracking the psychic link to a Morlock child, has to leave his chair and drag himself, and cuts his leg open without feeling it.

Talk about: Mikhail unlocking Iceman is the first hint of the far more powerful Iceman that Emma Frost drags out of him two years later. Xavier on the tunnel floor is a rare scene that treats the wheelchair as a real disability. The editor-in-chief credit is a Broadway gag, to match the Muppets title.

Uncanny X-Men #293
The Last Morlock Story!
X-Men Epic p. 143 · Oct 1992
writer Scott Lobdell · breakdowns Rurik Tyler · pencils Tom Raney · inks Joe Rubinstein · letters Lois Buhalis · colours Kevin Tinsley

Mikhail makes himself leader of the leaderless Morlocks and offers them the only peace he believes in: he opens the tunnels to the East River. Callisto goes for Xavier. Jean tells Warren the wings were never to blame — Apocalypse supplied the tools, but the choices were his. Iceman cannot hold back a river. Mikhail throws the X-Men clear and goes under with his people. Colossus phones his little sister in Russia. Storm finds her attic garden wrecked and Bishop, of all people, talks her through it.

Talk about: a mass suicide played straight in a 1992 X-Men comic — does Lobdell earn it? (They are not dead; the survivors come back as Gene Nation in 1995.) This is step one in breaking Colossus: his brother now, his parents and Illyana next year, and then he walks out to join Magneto.

X-Men #12 and #13
Broken Mirrors / Hazardous Territory
X-Men Epic pp. 166 and 188 · Sept–Oct 1992
writer Fabian Nicieza · art Art Thibert · art assist Dan Panosian (and Trevor Scott on #12) · colours Joe Rosas & Marie Javins · letters Tom Orzechowski (#12), Lois Buhalis (#13)

Carter Ryking, a mutant his own father kept locked in a private hospital in New Mexico, sees that father's death on CNN and breaks out. Wolverine, digging in Xavier's files, has found a folder claiming Xavier's father's atomic job was cover for government research into mutation. Xavier goes to the funeral in South Dakota; Carter — a childhood friend, now calling himself Hazard — takes him hostage and drags him to the ruins of the facility where both their fathers worked with Kurt Marko. The Blue team find a hidden sub-level, have a fight, and find nothing. Cutaways: a drawling caller who will not leave a message with Jubilee, and the Mutant Liberation Front fetching a relic for Stryfe.

Talk about: Nicieza's first issues as X-Men writer, and the first without Jim Lee — one of his inkers has been promoted to draw them. The comic spells the place Almagordo; the real town is Alamogordo. The whole plot is a loose end for sixteen years, until Mike Carey's X-Men: Legacy (2008) makes the facility Mister Sinister's project. The last panel of #13 announces the crossover in two weeks.

Catch-up: X-Force #1–15 in two minutes

You have not covered X-Force Epic 1, and the second volume assumes it. What matters:

  • The team is Cable's militarised New Mutants: Cannonball, Boomer, Warpath, Shatterstar, Feral, Siryn, with Sunspot and Rictor arriving late. The Domino who was there for most of it was a shapeshifting plant, Vanessa (Copycat), working for the arms dealer Tolliver; the real Domino was his prisoner. Vanessa turns up in hiding in X-Force #19.
  • Cannonball died and got up again. Cable's explanation is that Sam is an External, a mutant immortal, and the reason Cable came back in time at all. The Strike File and New Warriors #31 both lean on this.
  • Masque was killed in X-Force's fight with the Brotherhood — which is why the Morlocks fall apart in Uncanny #291–293.
  • Everybody is hunting Cable. SHIELD's G.W. Bridge and Canada's Kane (Weapon X) sent a squad called Weapon: PRIME after him. Both men are his old mercenary partners, and Blood & Metal is the story of why they hate him.
  • Stryfe has Cable's face. Readers saw it when he unmasked at the end of New Mutants #100 — you covered that in New Mutants Epic 8.
  • X-Force #15 ends with their base destroyed, Cable gone, and Cannonball leading the team on the run in a stolen SHIELD aircraft.
  • Behind the page: Liefeld drew his last issue with #9 and left for Image. Nicieza, who had been scripting, took over, and Greg Capullo arrived as artist with #15 — one issue before the crossover.
Cable: Blood & Metal #1 and #2
X-Force Epic pp. 5 and 49 · Oct–Nov 1992 · two double-size issues at $2.50
writer Fabian Nicieza · pencils John Romita Jr. · inks Dan Green · letters Bill Oakley · colours Brad Vancata

Then. Cable's mercenary crew — Bridge, Domino, Hammer, Grizzly, Kane — take jobs for Tolliver: Tehran, then the Khyber Pass, where they run into Stryfe. They start out as the Wild Pack and become the Six Pack after Symkaria objects to the name. In Uruguay, Stryfe has Kane and wants a stolen data disc. Hammer is about to hand it over. Cable shoots Hammer in the back and teleports out alone.

Now. The MLF are robbing museums for Stryfe — a broken sword, a shogun mask, a hieroglyph — all tied to Apocalypse. Cable ambushes them in Paris. Kane is sent after him, they fight, and then strike a deal: Stryfe for Cable, a cure for the paralysed Hammer for Kane. In the Yucatán Stryfe offers the same trade again — Kane's life for Cable's computer — and this time Cable picks his friend. Stryfe's power dwarfs his. Cable sees what is under the helmet, and the two of them escape into Cable's own future, where Kane gets new arms.

Talk about: Romita Jr. drawing peak-1992 hardware, with credits laid over maps and typed SHIELD memos. The Wild Pack rename is a real-world fix — Silver Sable already had a Wild Pack. The splash page carries a created-by credit for Rob Liefeld and Louise Simonson, months after Liefeld left. Marvel Age calls this the transition into the crossover: Cable is off in the future when somebody with his face shoots Xavier.

04The Song, chapter by chapter

A chapter a week from 1 September to 24 November 1992, by Marvel's own announcement, rotating Uncanny, X-Factor, X-Men, X-Force, three times round. Every chapter came sealed in a printed polybag with a trading card. The three Uncanny chapters carry musical titles: Overture, Familiar Refrain, Crescendo.

The plot in one breath. A man with Cable's face shoots Xavier with a techno-organic virus. In the confusion Mister Sinister has Cyclops and Jean abducted and sells them to Stryfe for a sealed canister. The X-Men and X-Factor hunt down X-Force as accomplices. Apocalypse, woken too early, is beaten half to death by Stryfe and ends up curing Xavier. Everybody goes to the Moon. Stryfe unmasks, calls Scott and Jean his parents, and vanishes with Cable into a time vortex. Sinister opens his canister and finds nothing in it but a cough.

1Uncanny X-Men #294
Overture
X-Men Epic p. 210 · X-Force Epic p. 93 · card: Xavier
writer Scott Lobdell · pencils Brandon Peterson · inks Terry Austin · letters Chris Eliopoulos · colours Mike Thomas

Lila Cheney plays a harmony concert in Central Park and Xavier makes an unscheduled speech about bigotry. Under the stage, men planning to kill him are killed first by someone who wants the job for himself. Xavier is shot on live television by what looks like Cable. Across town Apocalypse's Horsemen — Caliban now one of them — wreck Harry's Hideaway and take Scott and Jean. X-Force watch it on the news.

Talk about: Peterson's first issue. Xavier's speech names race, religion and sexuality one after another — in the autumn of an election, months after the Los Angeles riots. How does 1992's version of the mutant metaphor land now?

2X-Factor #84
Tough Love
X-Men Epic p. 234 · X-Force Epic p. 117 · card: Caliban
writer Peter David · pencils Jae Lee · inks Al Milgrom · letters Richard Starkings · editor Kelly Corvese

X-Force come to New York to find out whether Cable really did it, and run straight into the government's mutants. Havok lets them escape so he can follow them home. The hospital cannot slow the virus; Xavier is moved to the mansion. The Horsemen deliver Scott and Jean to their master — who is not Apocalypse at all but Mister Sinister wearing his face.

Talk about: Jae Lee. All shadow and scratch, nothing like the three artists around him. Best thing in the book or the chapter you dread? Peter David's jokes survive even here.

3X-Men #14
Fingers on the Trigger
X-Men Epic p. 258 · X-Force Epic p. 141 · card: Apocalypse
writer Fabian Nicieza · pencils Andy Kubert · inks Mark Pennington · letters Lois Buhalis · colours Marie Javins

In Panama, Sinister hands Scott and Jean to the MLF in exchange for a package from Stryfe. Apocalypse's followers pull him out of his healing sleep early, and he wakes weak and furious to learn a kidnapping has been done in his name. The Blue team and X-Factor find X-Force in west Texas.

Talk about: Andy Kubert's first X-Men — the credits box welcomes him aboard — and the start of a four-year run.

4X-Force #16
Jacklighting
X-Men Epic p. 281 · X-Force Epic p. 164 · card: Cable
writer Fabian Nicieza · pencils Greg Capullo · inks Harry Candelario · letters Chris Eliopoulos · colours Joe Rosas

The fight Marvel had been promising: the X-Men and X-Factor against X-Force on a Texas farm. The kids lose. Sinister appears — first as a hologram, then in person at the mansion — to admit he took Scott and Jean and to say someone else is running the game. In his Swiss safehouse, Cable, back from the future, works out who.

Talk about: who is in the right — the kids who went looking for answers, or the adults who arrest them off a news clip? Jacklighting is hunting at night with a lamp, to freeze the prey.

5Uncanny X-Men #295
Familiar Refrain
X-Men Epic p. 303 · X-Force Epic p. 186 · card: Bishop & Wolverine
Lobdell · Peterson · Austin · Eliopoulos · colours Joe Rosas

Apocalypse, in agony and delighted to be alive, flattens the X-team sent after him and then chooses not to kill them: somebody is playing him too. X-Force are locked up in the mansion. Cannonball trades what he knows about the MLF for a pardon and a place in the fight. Wolverine and Bishop slip away to Canada's Department K for Cable's file — and Cable is already in the building.

Talk about: Cannonball negotiating with Havok as an equal is the moment X-Force's leader stops being a New Mutant.

6X-Factor #85
Snikts and Bones
X-Men Epic p. 326 · X-Force Epic p. 209 · card: Polaris & Havok
writer Peter David · pencils Jae Lee · inks Al Milgrom · letters Richard Starkings · colours Glynis Oliver

Wolverine and Bishop against Cable among the filing cabinets, until he talks them round. Apocalypse searches Cable's safehouse, recognises his own technology in it, and reasons his way to who Cable must be. Stryfe starts work on Scott and Jean. The X-teams hit the MLF.

Talk about: Peter David pointed out at the time that the big fight in this issue of X-Factor has nobody from X-Factor in it. Apocalypse's deduction scene is the smartest two pages of the crossover — and the closest it comes to answering its own question.

7X-Men #15
The Camel's Back
X-Men Epic p. 349 · X-Force Epic p. 232 · card: the M.L.F.
writer Fabian Nicieza · pencils Andy Kubert · inks Mark Pennington · letters Chris Eliopoulos · colours Joe Rosas

The Mutant Liberation Front are taken apart. Beast and Moira get nowhere: the virus swallows every treatment they try. And Stryfe walks into Apocalypse's sanctum, goes through the Dark Riders, and introduces himself.

Talk about: the cover promises that Reaper kills Gambit and Quicksilver. He does not. Halfway through and the villain has only just met the other villain — is this where it sags?

8X-Force #17
Sleeping with the Enemy
X-Men Epic p. 373 · X-Force Epic p. 256 · card: Mister Sinister
writer Fabian Nicieza · pencils Greg Capullo · inks Harry Candelario · letters Chris Eliopoulos · colours Steve Buccellato

Stryfe against Apocalypse in Apocalypse's own temple, and Stryfe wins — for something Apocalypse will do to him centuries from now. The Dark Riders, raised on survival of the fittest, change sides. Aboard his space station Cable tells Wolverine and Bishop that Stryfe has his face and he does not know why. Apocalypse, jaw broken, teleports into the X-Mansion to ask for an alliance.

Talk about: Capullo cutting loose — a year later McFarlane hires him away for Spawn. The editor-in-chief credit here has DeFalco counting the money.

9Uncanny X-Men #296
Crescendo
X-Men Epic p. 398 · X-Force Epic p. 279 · card: Jean Grey & Cyclops
Lobdell · Peterson · Austin · Eliopoulos · colours Joe Rosas & Marie Javins

Stryfe's experiment on the couple he calls Mother and Father: he ties an infant's life to their escape, sure they will save themselves. They will not, and it shakes him. At the mansion Archangel has to vouch that Apocalypse can do what he claims — he once did this to Scott's baby son. Scott and Jean fight their way out of the complex and find out where they have been all along: the surface of the Moon.

Talk about: this is the heart of the thing. Is Cyclops a victim here, or a man finally being asked a fair question about the child he sent away?

10X-Factor #86
One of These Days… Pow! Zoom!
X-Men Epic p. 421 · X-Force Epic p. 302 · card: the Dark Riders
writer Peter David · pencils Jae Lee · inks Al Milgrom · letters Steve Dutro · colours Glynis Oliver

Apocalypse cures Xavier by forcing the virus to grow so fast it starves, while Archangel guards the door and everyone else is convinced he has been turned again. Xavier wakes. The teams take Apocalypse's ship to the Moon; Cable, Bishop and Wolverine go on ahead.

Talk about: the title is Ralph Kramden's threat from The Honeymooners — the one that ends on the Moon. The next-issue box announces a new X-Factor artist: Joe Quesada.

11X-Men #16
Conflicting Cathexes
X-Men Epic p. 444 · X-Force Epic p. 325 · card: Archangel
writer Fabian Nicieza · pencils Andy Kubert, who also inks pp. 20–23 · inks Mark Pennington · letters Chris Eliopoulos · colours Joe Rosas

Two strike teams storm the Moon base — Apocalypse's old one — against the Dark Riders, while Apocalypse hunts alone. Around Scott and Jean is a barrier that only Cable and Havok can walk through. Inside, Stryfe is waiting for Cable.

Talk about: the barrier is the clue — it lets through Summers blood. A cathexis is Freud's word for the emotional energy you invest in another person — Nicieza's title for a chapter about parents and children.

12X-Force #18
Ghosts in the Machine
X-Men Epic p. 467 · X-Force Epic p. 348 · card: Stryfe
writer Fabian Nicieza · pencils Greg Capullo · inks Harry Candelario · letters Chris Eliopoulos · colours Marie Javins

Archangel finds Apocalypse broken by his own Dark Riders and walks away rather than finish him. Stryfe takes off the helmet for Scott and Jean and accuses them of abandoning him. Cable hands Cyclops a trigger, drags Stryfe into a time vortex, and Scott has to press it. Both vanish. Scott is left believing he may have sacrificed his son a second time and will never know. Epilogue, a farmhouse in Nebraska: Sinister's man opens the canister that was supposed to hold the Summers bloodline. Empty. He has a cough.

Talk about: twelve chapters built on who Stryfe is, and it ends without saying. Cheat or the right call? The last two pages are the Legacy Virus being born — the editor-in-chief credit calls DeFalco the fat lady, because the song is over.

05Aftermath — X-Force Epic only

Stryfe's Strike File
X-Force Epic p. 373 · Jan 1993 · $1.75
writers Fabian Nicieza & Scott Lobdell · pencils mostly Larry Stroman, with Andy Kubert, Greg Capullo and Brandon Peterson · cover Andy Kubert & Mark Pennington

Xavier sits alone with a computer disc Bishop found on the Moon: Stryfe's private notes, thirty files of them. The trading cards in the polybags were cut-down versions of these. He reads it, decides nobody else should, and wipes it.

It is really the X-office's road map for 1993, hidden in plain sight. The Acolytes entry says an X-Man will join them of his own free will (Colossus) and that their saviour will rise again (Magneto). Illyana's says the plague will take the youngest (she dies in Uncanny #303). Beast's and Moira's say they are the ones who will have to fight it. Psylocke's asks why nobody questions what happened to Betsy Braddock (Revanche). And it introduces people who have not appeared yet: Graydon Creed, pointedly described as the only living child of the most savage mutant alive; Threnody; Siena Blaze; and a silent destroyer called Holocaust who never shows up as described — the name was recycled for Age of Apocalypse.

Talk about: the art credits include Jim Lee, Whilce Portacio and Marc Silvestri — three Image founders, in a Marvel comic dated January 1993, presumably on reused art. It also names Cable in full for readers, and calls Cannonball an immortal, which Marvel later quietly dropped.

New Warriors #31
Ruins
X-Force Epic p. 408 · Jan 1993
writer Fabian Nicieza · pencils Darick Robertson · inks Larry Mahlstedt · letters Joe Rosen · colours Joe Rosas · editor Rob Tokar

Subtitled a eulogy for the Hellions. Cannonball, Warpath and Firestar travel to Nova Roma to tell Magma and Empath that their old classmates are dead — killed by Fitzroy in Uncanny #281–282, your Epic 20. They find Empath holding the whole city under his control, because the truth was coming loose: Nova Roma was never a lost Roman colony. Selene kidnapped its people and rewrote them. Amara is really a British girl called Allison Crestmere.

Talk about: the only comic that stops to mourn the Hellions, and it is in a different title. That retcon was itself undone years later. Robertson went on to Transmetropolitan and The Boys. The Speedball, Nova and Namorita scenes are New Warriors business and can be skipped past.

X-Force #19
The Open Hand, the Closed Fist
X-Force Epic p. 433 · Feb 1993
writer Fabian Nicieza · pencils Greg Capullo · inks Harry Candelario · letters Chris Eliopoulos · colours Steve Buccellato & Marie Javins

X-Force have been well-treated prisoners at the mansion for nearly three weeks. Boomer redesigns everyone's costume. Sunspot talks to Stevie Hunter at the boathouse. Siryn and Warpath quietly copy twenty years of Xavier's files. Then Cannonball tells Xavier they are leaving, and makes his case: a closed fist can hit, but it can also warm, support and protect. Xavier lets them go — and lets Sam know he knows about the files. Lila Cheney teleports them out. Subplots: Vanessa in hiding in Massachusetts, and SHIELD arguing over the bill for hunting Cable.

Talk about: the new uniforms debut here, and the credits are written as school-yearbook jokes. The whole issue is talk — is it the best thing in the X-Force book? This is where X-Force stops being Cable's team and becomes Cannonball's.

06How it was made — from the back of the books

Both volumes reprint the same two documents, and together they are the best material you have. One is the sales pitch written before the crossover; the other is the confession written after.

Before: Marvel Age #116, September 1992

X-Men Epic pp. 492–497 · X-Force Epic pp. 457–462 · article by Steve Vrattos, cover by Andy Kubert

  • Bob Harras says the crossover began with Tom DeFalco walking into his office and telling him to do a big one for the autumn.
  • It was plotted at an X-Summit. The first of those, Harras says, was Claremont, Jim Lee and Portacio at San Diego, and it produced X-Men #1 — seven million copies by his count. This one was the new guys: David, Nicieza, Lobdell and the editors, in an Italian restaurant near the office and then crammed into DeFalco's own room because no conference room was free.
  • Peter David's verdict on the summit:
"It's the creative equivalent of root canal." Peter David, Marvel Age #116
  • The article never once says why there are new guys. Lee, Portacio and Liefeld are not mentioned as having left. Read it with that in mind.
  • It gets the future wrong. It announces Joe Quesada as the new Uncanny X-Men artist. That never happened — Peterson drew it, and Quesada went to X-Factor instead.
  • It also previews 1993: a Cable monthly by Nicieza and Art Thibert; mini-series for Sabretooth, Gambit and Deadpool (the last with a newcomer named Joe Madureira); a quarterly then called X-Men Spectacular, which shipped as X-Men Unlimited; and an Avengers crossover for the thirtieth anniversary.
  • Harras jokes that Nicieza is writing so many books they expect him to crack by December.

After: Nicieza's introduction, dated 3 February 1994

X-Men Epic pp. 502–503 · X-Force Epic p. 470 · written for the first trade paperback

  • He handed in the whole crossover as a single sheet of paper: a grid of twelve boxes, one per issue, with no title.
  • He cannot remember who named it, but he remembers the argument over spelling — X-ecutioner or X-cutioner — and says DeFalco's New York accent settled it.
  • What was cut: his outline gave Mister Sinister a much bigger part, and had Magneto come back from the dead in the middle of it. He calls that a kitchen-sink move born of nerves.
  • The best anecdote in either book: at one of these planning conferences Peter David shouted, as a joke, that Magneto should just rip out Wolverine's skeleton — and then watched the room take him seriously. That is the origin of Fatal Attractions, sitting in the back of this volume.
  • He lists what the crossover seeded: Illyana's death, the Legacy Virus, Magneto's return, the Betsy Braddock business, and a storyline he calls Fathers and Sons.
  • And he is candid about the result: it was unfair, he says, to expect a home run from a team at bat for the first time, and he would like to do it over.

The other extras

  • The twelve trading cards, front and back (X-Men Epic pp. 498–501, X-Force Epic pp. 464–467). Each is stamped Hunter or Prey: Xavier, Caliban, Apocalypse, Cable, Bishop & Wolverine, Polaris & Havok, the M.L.F., Mister Sinister, Jean Grey & Cyclops, the Dark Riders, Archangel, Stryfe. Did either of you buy these sealed?
  • X-Men Epic drops Jim Lee sketches from a Wizard hardcover between the early issues — a little like leaving his photo on the mantelpiece — plus a parody headache-pill ad starring Xavier from Marvel's 1992 Year in Review.
  • X-Force Epic has the Strike File's gold second-printing cover, Capullo's original art for the #19 cover, and two pieces from the Marvel Swimsuit Special.

07On the stands and in the world, autumn 1992

  • Image is eight months old. Youngblood in April, Spawn in May, Savage Dragon in July, Jim Lee's WildC.A.T.s in August — the same month as his last X-Men — and Cyberforce in October. Portacio's Wetworks was announced and then slipped to 1994.
  • Superman dies the week before the finale. Superman #75 went on sale 18 November 1992, also in a bag. Two publishers, same month, same trick.
  • Both cartoons arrive. Batman: The Animated Series started on 5 September; X-Men previewed on Halloween night. Marvel Age put the crossover and the cartoon on the same cover line.
  • At Marvel: Infinity War is finishing, the 2099 line launches with Spider-Man 2099 #1, and Spider-Man's thirtieth-anniversary banner is on some of the covers reprinted here. Regular issues are $1.25; the bagged chapters are $1.50.
  • At DC: Sword of Azrael is setting up Knightfall for the spring.
  • In the news: Clinton beats Bush and Perot on 3 November, three weeks before the finale. The Los Angeles riots were that spring. Anti-gay ballot measures were on the ballot in Colorado and Oregon.
  • The plague. 1992 is the year AIDS became the leading cause of death for American men aged 25 to 44. The Legacy Virus, planted on the last two pages, is the X-office reaching for that — and Lobdell had written Northstar's coming-out in Alpha Flight #106 only that March.
  • Everything else: Reservoir Dogs and Mortal Kombat in October; Aladdin, Bram Stoker's Dracula and Sonic 2 in November; Boyz II Men at number one for most of the run.

08Who made it

  • Scott Lobdell writes or scripts ten of the twenty-one issues in the X-Men book, plus the back-ups. He had only taken over Uncanny that year and stays until 1997. Harras's line on him in Marvel Age is that he had wowed them on Excalibur.
  • Fabian Nicieza writes eleven of the seventeen comics in the X-Force book. Born in Buenos Aires, he came up through Marvel's promotions department — he mentions it in his intro — co-created Deadpool, and had been writing New Warriors since its first issue. Here he inherits X-Force and X-Men in the same season.
  • Peter David had been writing X-Factor since 1991 and is the one voice here that pre-dates the exodus. He left the book with #89, three issues after this, mid-story — by most accounts because crossovers kept interrupting it. He died in May 2025.
  • Jim Lee and Whilce Portacio each draw their last regular X-Men comic in the first hundred pages. Lee now runs DC.
  • Brandon Peterson gets Uncanny for the crossover and a few issues after, co-creates Graydon Creed with Lobdell, and is replaced by John Romita Jr. at #300. He ends up at Top Cow.
  • Andy Kubert, son of Joe, starts on X-Men here and stays about four years.
  • Jae Lee is about twenty, coming off Namor. These three are his only issues of X-Factor. He is at Image within two years and wins an Eisner for Inhumans in 1999.
  • Greg Capullo came over from Quasar — Marvel Age says so — and leaves for Spawn at the end of 1993. Much later: Batman with Scott Snyder.
  • John Romita Jr. draws the Cable mini and returns to Uncanny at #300.
  • Tom Raney and Rurik Tyler are the fill-in team who end up drawing the whole Morlock story. Art Thibert was Jim Lee's inker, promoted for two issues and then given Cable #1. Mark Texeira draws the Maverick strips. Darick Robertson draws New Warriors #31.
  • Bob Harras edits all of it, and becomes Marvel's editor-in-chief in 1995. Tom DeFalco, who ordered the crossover, gets a different joke credit almost every issue.

09What it leaves hanging

  • The Legacy Virus runs for eight years. Illyana in 1993; Colossus dies curing it in Uncanny #390 (2001).
  • Who is the clone? Left open here on purpose. Cable #6–8, a year later, says Cable is the real Nathan and Stryfe the copy. Lobdell has since said the plan at the time was the other way round and that he argued Harras out of it, because it made Cyclops the father of a mass murderer. Then The Adventures of Cyclops and Phoenix (1994) sends Scott and Jean forward to raise him.
  • Apocalypse is left for dead on the Moon. Not for long.
  • Colossus: brother, parents, sister, then Magneto.
  • The Morlocks return as Gene Nation (Uncanny #323–325, 1995).
  • Storm and Forge stay broken for years; he ends up running X-Factor with Mystique on the team.
  • Iceman's real power — Emma Frost, Uncanny #314 (1994).
  • Dazzler's baby is later written off as lost. In 2013 Peter David, back on X-Factor, makes Shatterstar the son of Dazzler and Longshot.
  • Xavier's father and Alamogordo — X-Men: Legacy, 2008.
  • The nursing-home patient — the Phalanx Covenant, 1994.
  • X-Force on their own until Cable walks back in at #25.
  • Wolverine's skeleton — X-Men #25, October 1993.

10Questions to put to each other

  • Did the replacements pull it off? Rank the four art teams.
  • If a listener buys only one of these two books, which one — the X-Men volume for the lead-in, or the X-Force volume for the prologue and the fallout?
  • Twelve chapters. Where is the padding, and which one would you cut?
  • The story's only question is who Stryfe is, and it will not say. Does knowing the later answer change how chapter 12 reads?
  • Apocalypse as reluctant ally, with Archangel as his bodyguard — the best idea in the crossover?
  • X-Factor against X-Force: law and order against the kids. Whose side is the book on, and whose side are you on?
  • Jim Lee's last X-Men story is about Mojo. Fitting, or a waste of his final two issues?
  • Forge and Storm: who walked away from whom?
  • Is the Legacy Virus a good idea, knowing it runs eight years?
  • Polybags and trading cards: what did this era train readers to buy?

Three things to settle before you record.

First, the dinner scene in Uncanny #289: Bobby's father uses the word Oriental about Opal, and her relatives are cybernetic samurai. The scene knows he is a bigot — decide how you will say it out loud.

Second, if the X-Force back matter goes on screen, the two Swimsuit Special pages are in it (pp. 472–473).

Third, a few things here come from outside the books and are worth a second look before you state them flatly: the Steven Lang identification in #291, Lobdell's account of the clone switch, and the reason Peter David left X-Factor.
